# Does Jesus Believe in Equality?

Does Jesus believe in equality? That is one question a reader can't avoid after finishing the excerpts from Matthew and John. A lot of people would say no, and they'd point to how cold he is toward outsiders, but I think he does believe in it. The cold moments are the exception, not the rule.

The case against him isn't hard to build. When Jesus sends out the twelve, he tells them to "go nowhere among the Gentiles, and enter no town of the Samaritans" (Matt 10:5). Only Israel counts, it seems. Then a Canaanite woman shows up begging for her daughter, and "he did not answer her at all" (Matt 15:23). When she keeps pushing, he says "it is not fair to take the children's food and throw it to the dogs" (Matt 15:26). Reading that line, I'll admit it's hard to picture someone who sees all people as equal. He sounds like he has a list, and she isn't on it.

Still, I think the bigger picture points the other way. The same Jesus tells his listeners to "love your enemies," and his reason is that God "makes his sun rise on the evil and on the good, and sends rain on the righteous and on the unrighteous" (Matt 5:44-45). If God doesn't sort people, neither should we. He also asks what credit anyone gets for greeting only "your brothers and sisters," since "even the Gentiles do the same" (Matt 5:47). And the Canaanite story doesn't end with the dog comment. It ends with "Woman, great is your faith! Let it be done for you as you wish" (Matt 15:28). Her daughter is healed just like anyone else's would be. When a crowd wants to stone a woman for adultery, he tells them that only someone "without sin" can throw the first stone (John 8:7), which puts every person there on the same footing. Jesus starts with Israel, no question. But where he ends up is with everyone.
