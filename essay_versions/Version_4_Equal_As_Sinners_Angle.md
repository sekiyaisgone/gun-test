# Does Jesus Believe in Equality?

After reading the excerpts from Matthew and John, one question worth asking is whether Jesus believes in equality. Many readers say he does not, because he limits his mission to Israel and treats a Canaanite woman badly. I argue that he does, but his version of equality is not about who gets helped first. It is about everyone standing in the same place before God.

The doubters have a point. Jesus tells the apostles to "go nowhere among the Gentiles" (Matt 10:5). He says he "was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel" (Matt 15:24). He calls outsiders "dogs" (Matt 15:26). If equality means everyone gets treated the same way at the same time, then Jesus fails that test in these scenes. Even his own disciples want the woman gone, urging him to "send her away, for she keeps shouting after us" (Matt 15:23), and at first he seems to agree with them.

What I notice, though, is that Jesus keeps telling people they are no better than anyone else. When the scribes and Pharisees bring a woman caught in adultery and demand her death, he says, "Let anyone among you who is without sin be the first to throw a stone at her" (John 8:7). Every one of them walks away, "beginning with the elders" (John 8:9). Nobody in that crowd is above her. He tells his listeners to deal with "the log in your own eye" before pointing at "the speck in your neighbor's eye" (Matt 7:3). He even flattens the difference between his followers and the tax collectors they look down on: "if you love those who love you, what reward do you have?" (Matt 5:46). And the Canaanite woman ends up healed after all, with Jesus praising her: "great is your faith" (Matt 15:28). So yes, Israel comes first in the order of things. But in the way that matters most to Jesus, nobody outranks anybody.
