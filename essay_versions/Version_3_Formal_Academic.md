# Does Jesus Believe in Equality?

One of the questions raised by the excerpts from the Gospels of Matthew and John is whether Jesus believes in equality. Some readers conclude that he does not, because his mission is directed at a single people and he speaks dismissively of those outside it, but I argue that his teaching rests on the equal standing of all people before God, and that the passages which seem to say otherwise concern only the order in which his work unfolds.

The opposing reading has real textual support. Jesus instructs the twelve apostles to "go nowhere among the Gentiles, and enter no town of the Samaritans, but go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel" (Matt 10:5-6). When a Canaanite woman asks him to heal her daughter, he first ignores her, then states that he "was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel" (Matt 15:24), and finally compares her people to "dogs" who should not receive "the children's food" (Matt 15:26). A reader who stops at these lines would reasonably conclude that Jesus divides humanity into those who deserve his attention and those who do not.

The weight of the text, however, falls on the other side. Jesus commands his followers to "love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you," grounding the command in the fact that God "makes his sun rise on the evil and on the good, and sends rain on the righteous and on the unrighteous" (Matt 5:44-45). He warns against judging others at all, since "with the judgment you make you will be judged" (Matt 7:2). The golden rule, "in everything do to others as you would have them do to you" (Matt 7:12), admits no exceptions for nationality. Most tellingly, the Canaanite episode closes with him granting the request in full: "Woman, great is your faith! Let it be done for you as you wish" (Matt 15:28). The restriction to Israel governs where his mission begins, not who is worthy of it.
