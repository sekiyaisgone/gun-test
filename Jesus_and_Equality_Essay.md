# Does Jesus Believe in Equality?

One of the questions we might ask after reading the excerpts from Matthew and John is whether Jesus believes in equality. Many readers assume that he does not, since he tells his disciples to avoid non-Jews and even compares a foreign woman to a dog, but I argue that Jesus does believe in equality, and that his harsh moments are tests rather than his real position.

It is a common interpretation that Jesus puts his own people ahead of everyone else, and the text offers some support for that. When he sends out the twelve apostles, his first instruction is to "go nowhere among the Gentiles, and enter no town of the Samaritans, but go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel" (Matt 10:5-6). Later, when a Canaanite woman begs him to heal her daughter, "he did not answer her at all" (Matt 15:23). Once he speaks, he repeats that he "was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel" and adds that "it is not fair to take the children's food and throw it to the dogs" (Matt 15:24, 26). On its own, this sounds like someone who ranks people by origin.

I argue, however, that Jesus does believe in equality, and the rest of the text makes this hard to deny. He tells his followers to "love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you" because God "makes his sun rise on the evil and on the good, and sends rain on the righteous and on the unrighteous" (Matt 5:44-45). His rule for daily life has no exceptions written into it: "In everything do to others as you would have them do to you" (Matt 7:12). Even the story of the Canaanite woman ends with him giving in and praising her: "Woman, great is your faith! Let it be done for you as you wish" (Matt 15:28). This does not mean he treats every group the same way at every moment. His mission starts with Israel, but the equal worth of people before God is the idea he keeps returning to.
